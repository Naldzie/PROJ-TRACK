2. WHY WAS NEWTON-RAPHSON (CUBIC SOLVING) REMOVED?
================================================================================

  Original approach: Solve ax³ + bx² + cx + d = 100 via Newton-Raphson to
  find when progress hits 100%.

  Problem: For projects entering their rapid acceleration phase (mid-S-curve),
  the polynomial curve shoots upward steeply and predicts an over-optimistically
  early completion. A project that just entered its rapid growth phase would
  be predicted to finish very soon, which is unrealistic.

  Decision: Replaced with schedule-aware method for long-range estimates.
  Polynomial is preserved for short-range (next month) predictions only.


3. HOW THE SCHEDULE-AWARE METHOD WORKS (Step by Step)
================================================================================

  Industry Name: Earned Schedule-based Estimate at Completion (EAC_time)

  This method maps directly to Earned Value Management (EVM) - specifically
  the Earned Schedule variant of EAC:

    EVM Term                        | Our Implementation
    --------------------------------+-------------------------------------------
    Earned Value (EV)               | Current cumulative actual progress (%)
    Planned Value (PV)              | Baseline schedule planned progress (%)
    Earned Schedule (ES)            | Effective Position (interpolated month)
    Actual Time (AT)                | Current Month Index
    Schedule Variance (SV_time)     | Lag = AT - ES
    Schedule Performance Index (SPI)| ES / AT (implied by the lag)
    Estimate at Completion (EAC_time)| Total Planned Months + Lag
    --------------------------------+-------------------------------------------

  The difference from textbook EVM: we use direct interpolation against the
  baseline curve instead of a simple ratio (SPI = EV/PV), which gives a more
  precise lag calculation that respects the S-curve shape. This is a known
  refinement in EVM literature - not a deviation.

  Step 1 — Find effective position on the baseline curve:

    The system takes the current cumulative actual progress (e.g., 42%) and
    interpolates it against the planned baseline schedule to find where
    that progress level WAS SUPPOSED to occur according to the plan.

    Example:
      Baseline Month 5 = 30% planned progress
      Baseline Month 6 = 50% planned progress
      Actual progress   = 42%

      Effective position = 5 + (42% - 30%) / (50% - 30%) = 5.6 months

    Special cases:
      • If progress ≤ first baseline → effective position = 1.0
      • If progress ≥ last baseline → effective position = total months

  Step 2 — Compute lag:

    Lag = Current Month Index - Effective Position

    Example:
      Current project month = 8
      Effective position    = 5.6
      Lag = 8 - 5.6 = 2.4 months BEHIND schedule

    • Positive lag = behind schedule
    • Negative lag = ahead of schedule

  Step 3 — Project completion:

    Estimated Completion Index = Total Planned Months + Lag

    Example:
      Total planned months = 12
      Lag                 = 2.4
      Estimated index     = 12 + 2.4 = 14.4

  Step 4 — Convert index to date:

    Normal case (index within baselines):
      Interpolate between two adjacent baseline dates using the fractional
      part of the index.

    Past baselines (index exceeds all baselines):
      Add extra months to the last baseline's date, using 30 days per
      fractional month for the partial month.

  Step 5 — Compute delay in days:

    Predicted Delay Days = Estimated Completion Date - Planned End Date

    • Positive = behind schedule (shown in red)
    • Negative = ahead of schedule (shown in green)
    • Zero     = on schedule (shown in green)


4. EXAMPLE SCENARIOS
================================================================================

  Scenario A — Behind Schedule:

    Current month:     8
    Effective position: 5.6 (actual progress matches what was planned for month 5.6)
    Lag:               2.4 months behind
    Planned end:       Month 12
    Estimated end:     Month 14.4
    Result:            2.4 months / ~72 days behind

  Scenario B — On Schedule:

    Current month:     6
    Effective position: 6.0
    Lag:               0
    Planned end:       Month 12
    Estimated end:     Month 12
    Result:            On schedule

  Scenario C — Ahead of Schedule:

    Current month:     6
    Effective position: 6.8 (progress is ahead of the plan)
    Lag:               -0.8 months ahead
    Planned end:       Month 12
    Estimated end:     Month 11.2
    Result:            0.8 months / ~24 days ahead


5. CONFIDENCE CLASSIFICATION
================================================================================

  The polynomial regression R² is still used to determine how trustworthy
  the estimate is:

  High   → R² ≥ 0.85 AND observations ≥ 6 AND estimated index ≤ 1.5× planned
           (Green badge — reliable estimate)
  Medium → R² ≥ 0.70 AND observations ≥ 4
           (Yellow badge — reasonable estimate)
  Low    → Otherwise, or if estimated index > 2× planned
           (Red badge — rough estimate, strong disclaimer)

  If R² < 0.50 → return null (no estimate — "Not enough data")


6. STRENGTHS (For Capstone Defense)
================================================================================

  ✓ Defensible rationale: Clear explanation of why polynomial extrapolation
    was rejected (unrealistic over-optimistic predictions).

  ✓ Transparent and traceable: PMU users and evaluators can understand
    "you're 2.4 months behind the baseline curve, so completion shifts
    by 2.4 months." No black box.

  ✓ Grounded in real data: Uses the project's own baseline plan + actual
    progress records, not a curve that might behave unpredictably far
    beyond the data range.

  ✓ Confidence classification: Doesn't just dump a number — tells the
    user whether to trust it (High/Medium/Low).

  ✓ Conservative by design: Slightly overestimating delay is safer than
    promising early completion (which encourages complacency). The estimate
    self-corrects next month when new progress data is recorded.


7. HONEST LIMITATION (Be Ready to Address)
================================================================================

  Limitation: The method assumes the current lag stays constant — it's
  essentially "linear extrapolation of delay." If a project is currently
  behind but entering its rapid acceleration phase (mid-S-curve), the
  estimate might be too pessimistic because it doesn't account for the
  coming speed-up.

  Counter-argument for defense:

    "The schedule-aware method is intentionally conservative. For a
    decision-support system used by PMU managers, it is safer to slightly
    overestimate delay than to promise an early completion that encourages
    complacency. If the project accelerates, the estimate automatically
    corrects next month when new progress data is recorded. This is a
    deliberate architectural trade-off, not an oversight."

  KEY TAKEAWAY FOR DEFENSE
  ===============================================

  Our method is properly called **Earned Schedule-based Estimate at
  Completion (EAC_time)** — a recognized variant of the Earned Value
  Management (EVM) framework, which is the most widely accepted industry
  standard for construction project monitoring (PMI PMBOK, ANSI/EIA-748).

  The direct EVM mapping:

    EVM Element          → Our Implementation
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Earned Value         → Current cumulative progress (%)
    Planned Value        → Baseline planned progress (%)
    Earned Schedule (ES) → Effective Position (interpolated)
    Actual Time (AT)     → Current Month Index
    Schedule Variance    → Lag = AT - ES
    EAC_time             → Total Planned Months + Lag

  The refinement: textbook EVM uses a simple ratio (SPI = EV/PV), while
  we interpolate against the S-curve baseline for more precise lag
  calculation. This is a known enhancement in EVM literature, not a
  deviation from the standard.

  This makes the approach both:
    1. Industry-grounded — based on proven, auditable EVM principles
    2. Academically defensible — demonstrates understanding of standard
       project management frameworks and why pure polynomial extrapolation
       was deliberately rejected as over-optimistic

  CAN RANDOM FOREST / XGBoost WORK?
  ==================================

  Technically yes — but with two hard blockers:

  Feature set: ~15 features is workable for tree models (they handle mixed
  categorical/numerical data well). Issue types and severity being already
  implemented helps significantly.

  Possible target variables:
    • Binary classification — "Will this project need an extension?"
    • Regression — "Predicted delay in days"
    • Multiclass — "Risk level at completion" (Low/Medium/High/Critical)

  BLOCKER 1 — Insufficient Training Data
  ---------------------------------------

  The system is new. There are very few completed projects with known
  outcomes (delay, on-time, early). A reliable RF/XGBoost model needs
  dozens (preferably hundreds) of labeled historical records.

  The capstone itself acknowledges this limitation (Chapter 1, Scope):
    "The predictive analytics module depends on the availability, volume,
    and quality of historical project data provided by the PMU; therefore,
    the accuracy of delay estimation is constrained by the completeness
    and reliability of the dataset."

  Without sufficient training data, the model would overfit or produce
  unreliable predictions — which is worse than no prediction at all.

  BLOCKER 2 — Decision Support Philosophy (Client Requirement)
  -------------------------------------------------------------

  The capstone repeatedly defines the system as a transparent decision
  support tool:

  From Chapter 1:
    "The system is therefore not only a monitoring tool but also a
    decision-support mechanism that promotes proactive project management."

  From the project development rules:
    "The system is a Decision Support System. Its purpose is NOT to replace
    project managers. Its purpose is to provide early warning information.
    Final decisions always remain with the PMU."

  A Random Forest or XGBoost model is a black box — it cannot explain
  WHY it predicts a 2.4-month delay. It just outputs a number. This
  directly contradicts the client's requirement for explainability.

  The current polynomial + schedule-aware approach IS fully traceable:
  "Your actual progress is 42%. The baseline says 42% should have been
  at month 5.6. You are at month 8. So your lag is 2.4 months. Your
  completion shifts by 2.4 months." Every PMU manager can verify this.

  RECOMMENDED DEFENSE STATEMENT
  =============================

  If asked by your panel: "Why not use machine learning like Random Forest?"

    "We evaluated Random Forest and XGBoost during the design phase. Two
    factors made them unsuitable for this system.

    First, the system is new — it does not have enough completed projects
    with known outcomes to train a reliable model. As stated in our
    Chapter 1 scope, the accuracy of any prediction is constrained by the
    completeness of the dataset. A tree model trained on very few projects
    would overfit and produce misleading results. The PMU would need
    several years of data before this becomes viable.

    Second, and more importantly, the system is designed as a transparent
    decision-support tool — not a black box. The client requires that PMU
    managers can understand and verify every prediction. Our current
    approach — polynomial regression for next-month prediction and
    schedule-aware EVM-based estimation for completion date — is fully
    traceable. You can see exactly where the lag calculation came from.
    A random forest cannot explain why it outputs a specific number.
    That level of opacity is not appropriate for a government monitoring
    system where final decisions always remain with the PMU.

    We have flagged tree-based models as a future enhancement (V2 Phase 8)
    once sufficient project history accumulates. At that point, an ML
    model could supplement the transparent calculation with pattern-based
    insights."
