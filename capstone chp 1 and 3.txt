INTRODUCTION 
This chapter presents the foundation of the study. It discusses the context of the problem,

1.1 Project Context  
Project monitoring and management play a critical role in ensuring that development 
initiatives are implemented efficiently, transparently, and within the approved timeline and budget. 
Across various sectors worldwide, organizations implement infrastructure projects, system 
upgrades, and operational improvements that require continuous monitoring to ensure that project 
objectives are achieved. Effective project monitoring includes tracking project proposals, verifying 
milestone completion, maintaining organized documentation, coordinating with stakeholders, and 
generating progress reports that support managerial decision-making. In recent years, many 
organizations have shifted from traditional monitoring practices toward digital project management 
platforms.  
Globally, institutions increasingly adopt integrated information systems to improve 
transparency, reduce administrative workload, and enhance coordination between project 
stakeholders. Traditional approaches such as spreadsheet-based tracking, paper documentation, and 
email-based communication often lead to fragmented records, delayed updates, and difficulty in 
consolidating project information. These limitations make it challenging for administrators to 
monitor progress accurately and identify potential delays in a timely manner. As projects become 
more complex and involve multiple stakeholders, the need for centralized and automated 
monitoring systems becomes increasingly important. In the context of educational institutions, 
1 
infrastructure development and facility management projects are essential to support academic 
operations and campus expansion.  
Universities and colleges regularly undertake projects such as building construction, 
laboratory renovation, facility upgrades, and campus maintenance. Effective monitoring of these 
projects is necessary to ensure that resources are utilized properly and that project implementation 
aligns with institutional development plans. Without a structured monitoring system, institutions 
may encounter difficulties in tracking project progress, managing documentation, and maintaining 
communication between project administrators and contractors. At the institutional level, 
Camarines Sur Polytechnic Colleges (CSPC) manages various infrastructure and development 
projects through its Project Management Unit (PMU).  
The PMU is responsible for supervising the planning, documentation, monitoring, and 
implementation of institutional development projects. This includes evaluating project proposals, 
coordinating with contractors, ensuring compliance with design specifications, monitoring 
milestone completion, and maintaining official project records for administrative review. The PMU 
works closely with external contractors who are responsible for executing approved projects within 
the campus. Contractors typically prepare and submit project proposals for review and approval by 
the PMU.  
Based on a preliminary consultation with PMU personnel and the supervising engineer, it 
was identified that contractors currently submit their project proposals through email. After 
receiving these proposals, PMU personnel manually review the documents and encode the relevant 
information into internal records. However, the current process lacks a centralized digital system 
that can store, organize, and monitor project proposals and progress updates in real time. Because 
project proposals are transmitted through email and later transferred manually into records, the 
process can result in delays in encoding information, difficulty in consolidating project documents, 
and limited accessibility of project data for monitoring purposes.  
Furthermore, contractors do not directly input or modify project proposals within any 
institutional system. For confidentiality and data integrity reasons, only authorized PMU personnel 
are allowed to encode, edit, or update official project records. Contractors are limited to viewing 
the approved proposal details that correspond to their assigned projects. They are not allowed to 
modify proposals or access other administrative functions of the monitoring process. This 
restriction is implemented to ensure the protection of sensitive institutional information and 
maintain the accuracy of official project records. Because the monitoring process relies heavily on 
manual encoding and scattered documentation, PMU personnel must perform most monitoring 
tasks manually. Tracking project milestones, verifying updates from contractors, and generating 
progress summaries often require comparing multiple documents and communication records.  
Additionally, the absence of an integrated dashboard limits the ability of administrators to 
visualize real-time project progress. These operational conditions create several challenges for the 
PMU, including manual encoding of contractor-submitted proposals, delayed consolidation of 
progress reports, fragmented storage of project documents, limited real-time monitoring of project 
milestones, and the absence of predictive tools that can estimate potential project delays. Although 
contractors are involved in project implementation, their access to monitoring tools remains limited 
and not systematized, which further complicates coordination between project stakeholders. 
Without a centralized monitoring platform that clearly defines user roles and integrates project data 
into a unified system, project monitoring becomes reactive rather than proactive. As a result, 
administrators may encounter difficulties in identifying delays early, consolidating project 
information efficiently, and maintaining secure access control for different stakeholders. Therefore, 
the primary problem addressed by this capstone study is the absence of a centralized, role-based, 
and predictive project monitoring system that can streamline proposal management, regulate 
contractor access, and improve real-time monitoring of development projects within the CSPC 
Project Management Unit. 
1.2 Purpose and Description of the Project 
The purpose of this project is to design and develop PROJ-TRACK: An Integrated Monitoring and 
Management System for CSPC–Project Management Unit, a web-based platform intended to 
enhance the efficiency of project monitoring and management processes within the institution. The 
system is designed to centralize project-related information, including proposals, timelines, 
milestone updates, and supporting documents, into a single, organized database. Through this 
platform, authorized Project Management Unit (PMU) personnel will be able to record, update, and 
monitor project details in real time, while contractors will be provided with controlled access to 
view approved project proposals and monitor progress based on assigned permissions. The system 
also automates key processes such as notification and reporting, reducing reliance on manual 
tracking and improving overall operational workflow. 
The project offers several advantages in terms of efficiency, organization, and accuracy. 
By replacing manual and fragmented monitoring practices with a centralized digital system, PROJ
TRACK minimizes delays in report consolidation, reduces the risk of data inconsistency, and 
improves accessibility of project information. The inclusion of structured modules such as 
milestone tracking, document archiving, automated notifications, and dashboard visualization 
enables the PMU to efficiently manage multiple projects simultaneously. Additionally, the system 
enforces role-based access control, ensuring that only authorized personnel can modify sensitive 
project data while maintaining transparency for contractors through view-only access. These 
features collectively contribute to improved accountability, streamlined communication, and better 
coordination among stakeholders. 
A key differentiator of the proposed system is the integration of a predictive analytics 
module within a campus-based project monitoring platform. Unlike traditional systems that rely 
solely on reactive monitoring, PROJ-TRACK utilizes historical and real-time project data to 
estimate potential delays and timeline deviations. This predictive capability allows PMU personnel 
to anticipate risks and implement corrective actions before issues escalate. The system is therefore 
not only a monitoring tool but also a decision-support mechanism that promotes proactive project 
management. Its relevance lies in addressing the specific operational challenges of the CSPC
Project Management Unit, while its innovative approach demonstrates the practical application of 
regression-based predictive analytics in institutional infrastructure management.

1.5 Scope and Limitations of the Project 
This study covers the identification and analysis of the existing challenges in the project 
monitoring processes of the CSPC–Project Management Unit, including manual report 
consolidation, delayed contractor updates, fragmented documentation storage, and the absence of 
predictive monitoring tools. Based on the identified problems, the study includes the design and 
development of PROJ-TRACK, a centralized web-based integrated monitoring and management 
system. The system incorporates project registration, milestone tracking, contractor progress 
updates, document archiving, automated notification mechanisms, dashboard visualization, and a 
regression-based predictive analytics module for estimating potential project delays. Within the 
system, contractors are included as registered users with restricted access privileges. Contractors 
will submit project proposals through official email communication, and authorized PMU 
personnel will encode and manage the approved proposals within the system. Contractors will be 
granted view-only access to their respective approved project proposals and milestone status. They 
will not be permitted to edit, modify, or access administrative modules of the system in order to 
maintain confidentiality, data integrity, and institutional control over official records. The study 
further includes functional testing of the developed system using Black Box Testing to validate that 
all required features operate according to system specifications. Finally, the project covers the 
deployment of the system within the CSPC–Project Management Unit environment and its 
evaluation using ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards. The system will be accessible only to 
authorized PMU personnel and registered contractors during the implementation period. 
The system is limited to project monitoring and management functions within the CSPC
Project Management Unit and does not include financial accounting systems, procurement bidding 
automation, or integration with external government platforms. The predictive analytics module 
depends on the availability, volume, and quality of historical project data provided by the PMU; 
therefore, the accuracy of delay estimation is constrained by the completeness and reliability of the 
dataset. Contractor access is strictly limited to viewing approved proposals and monitoring project 
status; they cannot encode, edit, or manage system records. The system evaluation focuses on 
functional validation through Black Box Testing and quality assessment based on ISO/IEC 25010 
standards; other testing approaches such as performance stress testing or long-term operational 
evaluation are beyond the scope of this study. Additionally, the predictive model does not account 
for unforeseen external factors such as natural disasters, emergency policy changes, or sudden 
funding adjustments. All development, testing, and deployment activities are conducted within the 
academic timeframe, which limits large-scale expansion or multi-campus implementation. 
1.6 Project Dictionary 
This section defines key technical terms used in the study. Each term is presented with its 
conceptual definition based on established understanding and its operational definition as applied 
in this project.  
Artificial Intelligence. Artificial Intelligence refers to the capability of computer systems 
to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, and 
prediction [7]. In this study, Artificial Intelligence specifically refers to the forecasting mechanism 
integrated within PROJ-TRACK that utilizes historical project progress data to estimate potential 
delays. 
Black Box Testing. A software testing method that evaluates system functionality without 
examining internal code structure [6]. In this study, it refers to validating whether PROJ-TRACK 
features operate according to specified requirements. 
Functional Testing. Functional Testing is a type of software testing that verifies whether 
each function of a system operates in accordance with the specified requirements [1]. In this study, 
it refers to testing core system features such as user authentication, project tracking, notification 
triggering, contractor access, and predictive output. 
Integrated System. An integrated system combines multiple components into a unified 
platform that functions cohesively [2]. In this study, it refers to the centralized web-based platform 
that connects monitoring, documentation, notifications, reporting, and predictive modules. 
Integration Testing. Integration Testing is a testing approach where individual modules 
are combined and tested as a group to ensure they function correctly together [9]. In this study, it 
refers to verifying that all PROJ-TRACK modules operate seamlessly as one system. 
Performance Testing. Performance Testing is a type of testing conducted to evaluate the 
responsiveness, speed, and stability of a system under a given workload [4]. In this study, it refers 
to assessing how efficiently PROJ-TRACK processes data, generates reports, and displays 
dashboards. 
Predictive Analytics. Predictive analytics involves the use of statistical algorithms and 
data analysis techniques to forecast future outcomes based on historical data [11]. In this project, it 
refers to the analysis process used by the system to estimate potential project delays and projected 
completion timelines. 
Project Monitoring. Project monitoring is the continuous assessment of project activities 
to ensure alignment with planned objectives and timelines [10]. In this study, it refers to the digital 
tracking of milestones, contractor updates, and progress reports within PROJ-TRACK. 
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). A security mechanism that restricts system access 
based on user roles [12]. In this study, it ensures that contractors have view-only access while PMU 
staff retain editing privileges. 
Security Testing. Security Testing is a process used to identify vulnerabilities in a system 
and ensure that data and resources are protected from unauthorized access [3]. In this study, it refers 
to verifying that PROJ-TRACK enforces proper access control, particularly restricting contractors 
from modifying project data. 
Stress Testing. Stress Testing is a type of performance testing that evaluates how a system 
behaves under extreme conditions or heavy workloads [8]. In this study, it refers to assessing the 
system’s stability when handling large volumes of data or multiple simultaneous users. 
Usability Testing. Usability Testing is a method used to evaluate how easy and user
friendly a system is for its intended users [5]. In this study, it refers to assessing the interface, 
navigation, and overall user experience of PROJ-TRACK for PMU personnel and contractors

The study will utilize a mixed-method research design, which integrates both qualitative 
and quantitative approaches to provide a comprehensive understanding of the research problem [7].

explore existing workflows in depth while also evaluating system performance using measurable 
indicators [6]. Through this approach, both user experiences and technical performance will be 
considered in the development of the PROJ-TRACK system. 
For the first objective, which is to understand the current project monitoring and 
management process, qualitative techniques such as semi-structured interviews and direct 
observations will be employed. Interviews will be conducted with project management staff and

3.2.2 RESTful Architecture 
The system follows a RESTful architecture for handling communication between the front
end and back-end components. This approach uses standard HTTP methods such as GET, POST, 
PUT, and DELETE to perform operations on system resources. RESTful design ensures; efficient 
data exchange, scalability of the system, easy integration with other platforms (e.g., mobile 
applications or external APIs) [10]. This is particularly useful for PROJ-TRACK, as it supports 
real-time data updates and interaction between users such as PMU staff and contractors.

The PROJ-TRACK system incorporates a predictive analytics component powered by an 
AI API, specifically n8n workflow automation to support decision-making. This module analyzes 
historical project data such as timelines, progress rates, and completion patterns to identify potential 
risks and delays. The algorithm may include; trend analysis of project progress, comparison of

users, improved communication between stakeholders, and reduced delays in information 
dissemination. The integration with the Gmail API enables automated email notifications, ensuring 
that users are promptly informed of important project updates [3].

data. It stores essential information such as user accounts, project details, progress updates, reports,
